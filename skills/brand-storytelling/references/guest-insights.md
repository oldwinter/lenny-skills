## Andy Raskin
*Andy Raskin_*

> "This structure really is about defining a movement, and that's very different from, 'Hey, I'm going to solve your problem.'"

**洞察：** Strategic storytelling 聚焦 leading a movement，而不只是 solving functional problem。

**战术建议：**
- 将 brand framed as 通往 new way of winning 的 movement leader。

*时间戳：00:00:54*

---

> "The third piece is what I call naming the object of the new game... I find that what's the object of the new game really boils it down as kind of the rallying cry of the movement. So the example with Zuora, the object for a while was turn customers into subscribers."

**洞察：** Brand 需要一个 'rallying cry'，定义 new market reality 的 ultimate goal。

**战术建议：**
- 创建 'buyer mission statement' 或 rallying cry（例如 Airbnb 的 'Belong Anywhere'）。
- 尝试将 object of the game framed as question，以 engage prospect。

## Jason Feifer

## Matthew Dicks
*Matthew Dicks*

> "Every story is about a singular moment. I call it five seconds. It's a moment of either transformation, meaning I'm telling you a story about how I once used to be one kind of person and now I'm a new kind of person. Or more common is realization. Which is I used to think something and then some stuff happened and now I think a new thing."

**洞察：** 所有 effective stories 都 centered on 一个 singular、brief 的 internal change 或 realization moment。

**战术建议：**
- 识别 transformation 或 realization 发生的 'five-second moment'。
- 确保 story 的 98% 都提供 necessary context，让这个 moment 清楚。

*时间戳：00:05:28*

---

> "The dinner test is the idea that when you're telling a story in a formal way... the story that you're telling should be very closely related to the story you would tell someone if you were having dinner. So there should be no performance art included within your story."

**洞察：** Stories 应 natural and conversational，避免 unattributed dialogue 或 sound effects 等 'performance' tropes。

**战术建议：**
- 避免用 unattributed dialogue 或 sound effects（例如 'Bang!'）开头。
- 使用略微 elevated 的 natural dinner-table voice 讲述。

*时间戳：00:16:19*

---

> "I always say you should have, what I call an elephant at the beginning of the story... We have to immediately know that something is at stakes. We have to be worried about something."

**洞察：** Stories 必须立即 establish stakes，才能 capture and hold audience attention。

**战术建议：**
- 在最开头 plant an 'elephant'（clear problem 或 goal）。
- 使用 'backpack'：告诉 audience 你的 plan，这样当它出错时他们会担心。
- 使用 'breadcrumbs' drop hints，提示 future developments。
- 使用 'hourglass' 在最 critical moments slow down time。
- 使用 'crystal ball' 预测 false 或 frightening future，增加 tension。

*时间戳：00:25:43*

---

> "I just decided every day before I go to bed, I'm going to look back on the day and find one moment that would've been worth telling as a story. Even if it wasn't really worth telling, I was going to write it down. Now I don't write the whole thing down. That's crazy. It's not doable. What I do is I took an Excel spreadsheet, two columns, the date, and then I stretched the B column across."

**洞察：** 'Homework for Life' practice 会 build storytelling 的 'lens'，让你能在 everyday life 中找到 meaningful moments。

**战术建议：**
- 每晚花五分钟记录当天一个 story-worthy moment。
- 使用 simple spreadsheet，包含 date 和 moment 的 one-sentence description。
- 寻找 emotional resonance moments，即使它们看起来很小。

**洞察：** 用 specific location 和 immediate action 开场，可以 ground audience，并 signal narrative 正在开始。

**战术建议：**

- 确保 first sentence 包含 action，以 create momentum。
